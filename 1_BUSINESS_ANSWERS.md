## What is Caprae's mission?

Caprae's mission is to be the partner of choice for acquisition entrepreneurs. These are the searchers, operators and founders who want to buy, run and grow a lower-middle-market business. The point is to stay with them long after the deal closes, not just at the closing table. Kevin Hong founded the firm in 2024 after building and scaling technology companies himself. That background shows in how Caprae thinks about private equity. Most firms make their return at the moment of the transaction, through leverage and financial engineering. Caprae treats an acquisition as a seven-year journey and puts the emphasis on what happens after it: running the business better.

In practice the mission is a stack of capabilities built for people who have historically had very few. On the sourcing side, SaaSquatch Leads gives a searcher the kind of deal-flow engine that used to require an analyst team. On the services side, Search as a Service and M&A as a Service support buyers through seller outreach, diligence and financing. After the acquisition, Caprae's SaaS and operating support help portfolio companies adopt practical AI to make better decisions and run leaner. The common thread is giving small, ambitious teams the leverage that only large funds used to have.

The third part of the mission is cultural. Caprae describes itself as founder- and operator-first ("great founders working with great founders"). It has put real effort into governance and transparency in a corner of finance that rarely gets examined. Examples include its research on how often search fund CEOs are terminated, its Investor Rankings project for anonymous feedback on investor behavior, and the MBA Search Fund Alliance. These say the firm cares about how buyers and investors treat the people who actually run the companies.

Put simply: Caprae wants to make acquisition entrepreneurship more accessible, more operator-driven and more honest, and to build its own technology to make that possible.

## How is Caprae changing the ETA space and broader PE?

The traditional search fund model has barely changed in about forty years. A searcher raises capital from investors, then spends up to two years and much of that money cold-calling business owners, often without closing a deal. If they do buy a company, they can be replaced as CEO by the same investors. The most expensive and failure-prone part of that model is sourcing, and that is where Caprae starts. SaaSquatch turns weeks of manual list-building into a tool priced like software, and Search as a Service turns the rest of the search into something a buyer can subscribe to instead of building from scratch.

Lowering the cost of searching changes who can search. When sourcing and outreach become affordable services, ETA is no longer reserved for people who can raise a traditional search fund. It opens up to MBAs, career changers, independent sponsors and international buyers. Caprae's growth, with a meaningful share of its clients based outside the United States, suggests that demand was there all along. The infrastructure just did not exist.

Caprae is also pushing the industry on governance, which is where it has been most willing to be uncomfortable. Its research found that Black search fund CEOs are terminated at roughly three times the rate of their peers, despite being only a small share of those funded. It stress-tested published ETA research against termination outcomes, and it launched Investor Rankings so searchers can see how investors actually behave. Few firms publish data that makes their own industry look bad. Doing so pressures investors to treat CEOs as partners rather than replaceable hires.

For private equity more broadly, Caprae is a working example of a different model. Value comes from operating improvements and technology after the acquisition, not from leverage at the moment of the deal. The firm builds its own software, uses AI both to find deals and to improve the companies it backs, and runs a global talent engine that develops people internally. If that model keeps compounding, lower-middle-market PE will start to look more like a technology company than a traditional fund. That is the shift Caprae is betting on.
